# Feed Efficiency and Methane Reduction (MBT55)

MBT55 improves feed efficiency and reduces methane emissions by optimizing  
fermentation, enhancing SCFA production, stabilizing gut microbiota,  
and reducing hydrogen availability for methanogenesis.  
This creates a high-performance, climate-positive livestock system.

---

# 1. Overview

Feed inefficiency and methane emissions are driven by:

- Poor fiber digestion  
- Excess hydrogen production  
- Methanogenic archaea dominance  
- Gut dysbiosis  
- Stress and low-quality feed  

MBT55 corrects these through microbial and metabolic optimization.

---

# 2. Fermentation Optimization

MBT55 enhances:

- Fiber degradation  
- Protein utilization  
- VFA/SCFA production  
- Energy extraction  

This increases **feed conversion efficiency (FCE)**.

---

# 3. SCFA Pathways

SCFA provide:

- 60–70% of ruminant energy  
- Improved gut barrier function  
- Reduced inflammation  
- Better nutrient absorption  

MBT55 boosts SCFA-producing microbes.

---

# 4. Methane Reduction Mechanisms

## 4.1 Hydrogen Competition
MBT55 reduces hydrogen availability by:

- Increasing SCFA pathways  
- Enhancing lactate–propionate conversion  
- Supporting alternative electron sinks  

Less hydrogen → less methane.

---

## 4.2 Methanotroph Activation
MBT55 stimulates:

- Type I methanotrophs  
- Type II methanotrophs  

These microbes **consume methane**.

---

## 4.3 Redox Cycling
Mn/Fe cycling:

- Competes with methanogenesis  
- Reduces anaerobic hotspots  

---

# 5. Expected Outcomes

- Lower methane emissions  
- Higher feed efficiency  
- Improved weight gain  
- Reduced odor  
- Better gut stability  

---

# 6. PBPE Integration

Feed efficiency & methane reduction become PBPE value:

- MRC（Methane Reduction Credit）  
- FEC（Feed Efficiency Credit）  
- LHC（Livestock Health Credit）  

---

# 7. One-Sentence Summary

**MBT55 improves feed efficiency and reduces methane by optimizing fermentation,  
boosting SCFA pathways, and suppressing methanogenesis through microbial  
and redox-driven mechanisms.**
