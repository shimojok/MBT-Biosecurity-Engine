# Soil Regeneration Engine (MBT55)

The Soil Regeneration Engine describes how MBT55 restores degraded soils  
through microbial balance, humification, redox cycling, and structural improvement.  
It transforms soils from low‑carbon, pathogen‑prone systems into  
stable, fertile, carbon‑rich ecosystems.

---

# 1. Overview

Soil degradation is driven by:

- Loss of organic matter  
- Microbial collapse  
- Compaction  
- Erosion  
- Chemical overuse  
- Salinity and acidity  
- Pathogen accumulation  

MBT55 reverses these processes through ecological regeneration.

---

# 2. Microbial Restoration

MBT55 restores microbial diversity by:

- Reintroducing beneficial microbes  
- Balancing aerobic/anaerobic ratios  
- Increasing SCFA production  
- Suppressing pathogens  
- Enhancing nutrient cycling  

This creates a **stable microbial foundation**.

---

# 3. Organic Matter Transformation

MBT55 accelerates decomposition of:

- Crop residues  
- Manure  
- Organic waste  
- Root exudates  

Outputs:

- Organic acids  
- Fulvic acids  
- Humic substances  

These form the basis of soil carbon.

---

# 4. Redox Cycling

Redox-active microbes drive:

- Mn/Fe cycling  
- Electron shuttling  
- Oxidative stabilization  

Benefits:

- Pollution transformation  
- Improved nutrient availability  
- Enhanced carbon stabilization  

---

# 5. Soil Structure Improvement

MBT55 increases:

- Aggregation  
- Porosity  
- Water retention  
- Root penetration  

This reduces:

- Compaction  
- Erosion  
- Drought stress  

---

# 6. Pathogen Suppression

Through antagonism and niche exclusion:

- Fusarium  
- Pythium  
- Phytophthora  
- Rhizoctonia  

are suppressed naturally.

---

# 7. PBPE Integration

Soil regeneration outputs become PBPE value:

- Soil Carbon Gain → ΔC‑SCC  
- Soil Health Credit → SHC  
- Disease Reduction → DRC  

---

# 8. One-Sentence Summary

**MBT55 regenerates soil by restoring microbial balance, accelerating humification,  
improving structure, and stabilizing carbon, transforming degraded soils into  
resilient, fertile ecosystems.**
