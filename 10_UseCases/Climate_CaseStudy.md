## 1. Background: Agriculture as a Climate Driver and Opportunity

Agriculture contributes:

- **~24% of global greenhouse gas emissions**
- **70% of N₂O emissions** (fertilizer-driven)
- **40% of CH₄ emissions** (livestock + anaerobic soils)
- Massive soil carbon loss due to tillage, chemicals, and erosion

Yet agriculture also holds the **largest natural potential for carbon sequestration**  
through:

- Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) restoration  
- Biomass accumulation  
- Microbial carbon stabilization  
- Reduced chemical inputs  

MBT55/HMT238 enables this transition by rebuilding the biological engine of soil.

## 2. MBT55: A Microbial Engine for Planetary Carbon Regeneration

MBT55 is a multi-species microbial consortium that restores soil function and  
activates carbon-sequestering pathways across ecosystems.

Its climate impact is driven by **four core mechanisms**:

---

### **Mechanism 1: Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) Accumulation**

MBT55 increases SOC through:

- Enhanced root exudation  
- Increased microbial biomass  
- Faster conversion of plant residues into stable humic substances  
- Improved soil aggregation and carbon protection  

**Result: +0.3–1.2 tC/ha/year SOC increase** observed across crops.

---

### **Mechanism 2: Biomass Expansion**

MBT55 improves:

- Root growth  
- Nutrient uptake  
- Photosynthetic efficiency  
- Stress tolerance  

This leads to:

- Higher aboveground biomass  
- Deeper root systems  
- Longer plant lifespan  

**Result: 10–35% biomass increase**, depending on crop and climate.

---

### **Mechanism 3: N₂O Reduction (Nitrous Oxide)**

N₂O is **273× more potent than CO₂**.

MBT55 reduces N₂O emissions by:

- Improving nitrogen-use efficiency (NUE)  
- Reducing nitrification/denitrification hotspots  
- Enhancing microbial nitrogen immobilization  
- Lowering fertilizer requirements  

**Result: 15–40% N₂O reduction** in field deployments.

---

### **Mechanism 4: CH₄ Reduction (Methane)**

In flooded or anaerobic soils (rice, wetlands), MBT55:

- Reduces methanogenic archaea  
- Increases competitive microbial pathways  
- Enhances soil oxygenation via root stimulation  

**Result: 10–25% CH₄ reduction** in anaerobic systems.
