# What Is Biosecurity?

Biosecurity is the set of biological, ecological, and operational measures  
that protect crops, livestock, food systems, soil, water, and human health  
from biological threats, instability, and loss.

It is not limited to disease control.  
Modern biosecurity integrates:

- Microbial ecology  
- Soil and climate stability  
- Food system resilience  
- One Health  
- Supply‑chain continuity  
- Economic and environmental risk reduction  

Biosecurity = **Biology × Stability × Value**

---

## 1. Why Biosecurity Matters

Global food systems face increasing risks:

- Climate‑driven disease expansion  
- Soil degradation  
- Post‑harvest spoilage  
- Livestock disease outbreaks  
- AMR (antimicrobial resistance)  
- Supply‑chain volatility  
- Price instability  

Biosecurity provides the **first line of defense** and the **foundation of resilience**.

---

## 2. Biosecurity as an Ecological System

Biosecurity is not only “protection from pathogens.”  
It is the **active engineering of ecological stability**.

Key components:

- Microbial balance  
- Priority effect (first occupancy)  
- Antagonism and niche exclusion  
- Soil carbon stabilization  
- Climate gas suppression (CH₄, N₂O)  
- Food loss reduction  
- Immune activation (ISR, One Health)  

Biosecurity is achieved when ecosystems become **self‑stabilizing**.
